Manila · SM North EDSA & TriNoma

No single post moved the crowd enough for us to see it.

We lined up anonymised movement across two of Manila's biggest malls against every social post we could find. No individual post produced a mall-wide footfall response we could distinguish from normal variation — yet the same data clearly registers a mall-scale change when one happens. (Smaller or single-tenant effects can sit below what a location panel this size can resolve.) Here's the evidence.

01

What can this data actually resolve?

—

Before asking whether posts worked, know the instrument's resolution. A location panel is a sample: day-to-day it wobbles by hundreds of shoppers on its own. An effect has to clear that wobble to be visible.

Pooled across every event — the most powerful test
…and the averaged footfall path, day −7 to +7
Minimum detectable effect — 7-day campaign, cross-mall share of dwell ≥10 min visitors, 80% power

This is a campaign-scale detection floor. It is different from the per-day ±2σ band used later to flag one unusual day: a single day is far noisier than a 7-day window, so it needs a much bigger move to stand out.

02

Proof the instrument works: the renovation

—

While TriNoma was being renovated (Feb–May 2026), SM North's share of the two malls rose from 54.6% to 56.5% — i.e. TriNoma lost about 2 points of share while it was disrupted — then moved back toward baseline (55.0%) in the short 23-day post-period. The same system that finds no signal from individual posts clearly registers this mall-scale change.

Read shares carefully. The two malls are physically linked across EDSA, so this share partly reflects visitors substituting between them. A campaign that grew the two malls' combined traffic would not show up here at all — share only sees one mall gaining relative to the other. We use share because raw counts aren't comparable across the year (the device panel shrinks over time).
